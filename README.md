Quark Interactions and the Formation of Stable Matter

Exploring Particle Structure Through a Process-Based Framework

Author: Sanjay Soni
Independent Researcher — Conceptual Science & Philosophy
Research Framework: Gati-Pravah Siddhant (Action-Flow Theory)

Research Status: Conceptual research questions for scientific examination; not an established physical theory.

Abstract

Elementary particles and their interactions form the foundation of modern particle physics. Protons and neutrons are composite particles whose internal structures involve quarks and gluons, described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

This paper explores whether the formation and stability of composite particles can also be examined through a general process-based perspective involving physical conditions, interactions, changes, and the emergence of stable structures.

The central question is whether such a conceptual framework can provide a useful additional description without replacing or contradicting established particle physics.

The paper identifies questions about quark confinement, gluon-mediated interactions, energy, and the organization of matter. It invites independent researchers to identify the mathematical tools and experimental evidence required to evaluate the framework.

1. Introduction

Matter exists in different levels of organization, from elementary particles to atoms, molecules, and biological systems.

Modern physics describes these levels using different theories and mathematical models. At the subatomic level, quantum chromodynamics explains the strong interaction involving quarks and gluons.

My research interest is whether the processes that produce and maintain structures can be considered within a common conceptual sequence:

Physical Conditions → Interaction → Change → New State → Structural Stability

I refer to this process-based perspective as Gati-Pravah Siddhant, or Action-Flow Theory.

This sequence is a conceptual proposal. It does not imply that every physical interaction is identical or that all interactions are governed by one known force.

2. Established Scientific Background

Quarks are elementary particles in the Standard Model. They carry color charge and participate in the strong interaction.

Gluons mediate the strong interaction between quarks. Protons and neutrons are composite particles containing three valence quarks, together with gluon fields and additional quark-antiquark contributions arising in their quantum description.

Under ordinary conditions, quarks are not observed as isolated free particles. This phenomenon is known as quark confinement.

A proton contains two up valence quarks and one down valence quark. A neutron contains one up valence quark and two down valence quarks.

Their different electric charges arise from the electric charges assigned to their constituent quarks.

These facts are established parts of modern particle physics. The present paper does not propose to replace them.

3. The Central Research Question

How do interactions among quarks and gluons produce the properties, internal structure, and stability of composite particles, and can a process-based description help clarify this relationship?

This question has several parts:

1. How does the strong interaction produce quark confinement?
2. How do gluon fields contribute to the internal structure and energy of protons and neutrons?
3. How do the properties of constituent quarks relate to the overall properties of composite particles?
4. Can a general description involving interactions, energy, and structural change provide useful insights beyond a verbal summary of existing physics?

4. Connection With Action-Flow Theory

The conceptual framework proposes examining physical processes through four connected questions:

- What physical conditions are present?
- Which interactions occur?
- What changes follow from those interactions?
- What properties characterize the resulting state?

Applied to particle physics, these questions encourage a systematic examination of the relationship between interactions and the resulting structure.

However, the framework must distinguish between a general description of a process and a mathematical explanation of its mechanism.

For example, saying that quark interactions contribute to a proton's structure is consistent with established physics. Claiming that a new, universal motion-flow mechanism explains quark confinement would require a precise mathematical formulation and independent evidence.

5. Research Questions for Further Investigation

The following questions are proposed for scientific discussion:

Question A: Can the physical origin of quark confinement be described in a way that offers additional insight into the relationship between interaction and stable structure?

Question B: How do the strong interaction, gluon fields, and quantum energy contribute to the observable properties of protons and neutrons?

Question C: What mathematical concepts would be necessary to express a process-based model of particle structure?

Question D: What measurable prediction could distinguish such a model from the existing QCD description?

6. Proposed Research Method

This paper is conceptual and does not report a new experiment or calculation.

A possible research programme would involve:

1. Reviewing introductory and advanced literature on quantum chromodynamics.
2. Identifying the established mathematical description of quark confinement.
3. Defining the terms interaction, motion, energy, flow, and structure precisely.
4. Determining whether the proposed framework contains any relationship not already represented in existing equations.
5. Developing a mathematical model if a distinct and meaningful hypothesis can be identified.
6. Comparing any predictions with existing observations or suitable computational results.

The framework should be revised or rejected wherever it conflicts with reliable evidence without providing a demonstrably better explanation.

7. Limitations

This paper does not establish a new mechanism for quark confinement.

It does not claim that gluons are electromagnetic particles, that all interactions are the same, or that motion alone explains the internal structure of matter.

The proposed process-based framework currently serves as a conceptual approach to formulating research questions.

8. Relationship to the Next Papers

This first paper examines the formation and stability of composite particles.

The second paper extends the investigation to the conditions under which hadrons and atomic nuclei emerged during the early evolution of the universe.

The third paper investigates electric charge, including the relationship between the opposite charges of electrons and protons.

Together, the papers explore three connected levels: particle structure, cosmic evolution, and electric charge.

In particular, I would appreciate references to relevant literature, explanations of established results, and suggestions for developing a mathematically testable hypothesis.

Early Cosmic Conditions and the Formation of Nuclear Structure

Investigating the Physical Processes Before and During the Formation of Atomic Nuclei

Author: Sanjay Soni
Independent Researcher — Conceptual Science & Philosophy
Research Framework: Gati-Pravah Siddhant (Action-Flow Theory)

Research Status: Conceptual research inquiry based on established cosmological knowledge and open questions.

Abstract

The early universe underwent substantial physical changes as it expanded and cooled. During its evolution, quarks and gluons became confined within hadrons, including protons and neutrons. Later, nuclear reactions produced light atomic nuclei, including helium nuclei.

Although major stages of this evolution are supported by established physical models and observations, questions remain about the earliest conditions of the universe and the limits of our knowledge concerning its initial stages.

This paper asks whether the sequence of changing physical conditions, interactions, and emerging structures can be investigated through the process-based perspective of Gati-Pravah Siddhant (Action-Flow Theory).

The purpose is not to claim that nuclear formation occurred through an unknown force. Instead, the paper distinguishes established stages of cosmic evolution from unresolved questions and identifies possible directions for further investigation.

1. Introduction

The universe contains structures that exist at very different physical scales.

The formation of these structures depends on physical conditions and interactions. However, the relevant mechanisms differ across energy scales and physical environments.

In the early universe, high temperatures and densities permitted physical states that are not normally found under everyday conditions. As the universe expanded and cooled, different particles and structures became possible.

This raises a fundamental question:

How did changes in the physical conditions of the early universe lead to the emergence of the particles and nuclei that later became the building blocks of ordinary matter?

The question can be examined using established cosmology, nuclear physics, and particle physics.

2. What Is Already Established?

The broad sequence of early cosmic evolution includes several important stages.

Stage 1: Early hot universe

The universe passed through an extremely hot and dense early state. The physics of its earliest moments remains an active research area, particularly where quantum gravity may be relevant.

Stage 2: Quarks and gluons

At sufficiently high temperatures, matter can exist in a state in which quarks and gluons are not confined within ordinary individual hadrons.

Stage 3: Formation of hadrons

As the universe cooled, quarks became confined into hadrons, including protons and neutrons.

Stage 4: Big Bang nucleosynthesis

During the early universe, nuclear reactions produced light nuclei, particularly helium-4, along with deuterium, helium-3, and trace amounts of other light nuclei. Hydrogen nuclei, which are protons, also remained abundant.

Stage 5: Formation of neutral atoms

Much later, electrons combined with nuclei to form neutral atoms as the universe cooled sufficiently. This stage is known as recombination.

These stages are not interchangeable. Hadron formation, nuclear formation, and neutral atomic formation are distinct physical processes.

3. The Central Research Question

What physical conditions and interactions preceded the formation of atomic nuclei, and how can the transition from the early universe to increasingly complex structures be understood within a coherent process-based framework?

This question includes several more specific questions:

1. What can established observations tell us about the physical state of the universe before hadron formation?
2. How did the transition from quarks and gluons to protons and neutrons occur?
3. What conditions allowed light nuclei to form?
4. Which aspects of the earliest cosmic conditions remain uncertain?
5. Can a process-based framework provide a useful additional description of these transitions?

4. The Action-Flow Perspective

Gati-Pravah Siddhant proposes examining the relationships among physical conditions, interactions, and changes in state.

For early cosmic evolution, this can be expressed conceptually as:

Initial Physical Conditions → Expansion and Cooling → Changes in Particle States → Hadron Formation → Nuclear Reactions → New Cosmic Conditions

This sequence is intended as a descriptive framework, not a replacement for the equations governing cosmological expansion, quantum chromodynamics, or nuclear reactions.

The framework must account for the fact that different stages involve different physical mechanisms.

For example, quark confinement is associated with the strong interaction, while the formation of light nuclei involves nuclear reactions. The formation of neutral atoms later involves electromagnetic interactions between electrons and nuclei.

5. The Unresolved Question of What Came Before

A distinction is necessary between three different questions:

- What happened before atomic nuclei formed?
- What happened before protons and neutrons formed?
- What, if anything, preceded the earliest physical state described by our current cosmological models?

The first two questions can be investigated through established models of early-universe particle physics and nuclear physics.

The third is more difficult. The standard cosmological model describes the universe's evolution from an early hot, dense state, but a complete, experimentally confirmed account of the ultimate beginning is not currently available.

It would therefore be inaccurate to say that nothing is known about the period before nuclear formation. Significant evidence exists for many stages of early cosmic evolution.

The scientific challenge is to identify precisely which earlier conditions can be reconstructed and which remain uncertain.

6. Proposed Research Directions

The following research directions could help develop this inquiry.

A. Study of established cosmic transitions

Examine the physics of quark confinement, hadron formation, and Big Bang nucleosynthesis.

B. Investigation of physical conditions

Identify how temperature, density, expansion, and interaction rates affect the formation and survival of different structures.

C. Comparison with observations

Study how the observed abundance of light elements and the cosmic microwave background constrain models of early cosmic evolution.

D. Development of a process-based model

Determine whether Gati-Pravah Siddhant can express any relationship more clearly or predict an outcome not already captured by established models.

7. Limitations

This paper does not propose a verified alternative to the Big Bang model.

It does not claim that hydrogen or helium formed solely through electromagnetic interactions.

The formation of helium nuclei requires nuclear reactions. Electromagnetic interactions are important to the properties and behavior of charged particles, but they do not replace the nuclear processes involved in fusion.

The framework remains conceptual until its terms, equations, and testable predictions are specified.

8. Relationship to the First and Third Papers

The first paper asks how quarks and gluons contribute to the structure of protons and neutrons.

This second paper considers how the physical conditions of the early universe permitted these particles and later atomic nuclei to form.

The third paper examines electric charge and asks why electrons and protons possess equal-magnitude but opposite-sign charges.

These questions are related, but each requires its own scientific explanation and evidence.

Electric Charge and the Relationship Between Electrons and Protons

A Fundamental Inquiry Into Charge, Matter, and the Origins of Physical Properties

Author: Sanjay Soni
Independent Researcher — Conceptual Science & Philosophy
Research Framework: Gati-Pravah Siddhant (Action-Flow Theory)

Research Status: Conceptual research questions open to mathematical analysis and independent scientific evaluation.

Abstract

Electric charge is a fundamental property of elementary particles and determines how they participate in electromagnetic interactions.

Electrons carry negative electric charge, while protons carry positive electric charge. The magnitudes of their charges are equal, although their signs are opposite. This relationship is essential to the electrical neutrality of ordinary atoms.

Modern physics describes electromagnetic interactions through quantum electrodynamics and incorporates electric charge into the Standard Model of particle physics.

This paper asks why the observed charge assignments exist, how they relate to the internal structure of protons, and whether the relationship between positive and negative charge can be investigated through a broader process-based framework.

The paper does not assume that the electron's charge originates from the proton, nor does it claim that the origin of electric charge has already been explained by Action-Flow Theory. It identifies questions requiring further theoretical and scientific investigation.

1. Introduction

Electric charge is among the basic properties used to describe matter and its interactions.

A proton carries positive electric charge, an electron carries negative electric charge, and a neutron has zero net electric charge.

The electron and proton have equal charge magnitudes:

Electron charge: −e
Proton charge: +e

Here, e denotes the positive elementary charge.

In an electrically neutral atom, the total positive charge of its protons balances the total negative charge of its electrons.

This precise relationship raises a natural question: why do the electron and proton have equal-magnitude but opposite-sign charges?

The question concerns the fundamental structure of physical laws rather than simply the behavior of atoms.

2. Established Scientific Background

Electric charge is a property that determines how particles participate in electromagnetic interactions.

The electromagnetic interaction is described with great precision by quantum electrodynamics (QED), while the Standard Model provides a broader framework for the known elementary particles and their interactions.

The electron is an elementary particle in the Standard Model.

The proton is a composite particle containing two up valence quarks and one down valence quark, together with gluon fields and additional quantum contributions.

The up quark carries electric charge +2e/3, and the down quark carries electric charge −e/3.

Consequently, the total charge associated with the proton's valence-quark composition is:

(+2e/3) + (+2e/3) + (−e/3) = +e

For the neutron, the corresponding valence-quark composition gives:

(+2e/3) + (−e/3) + (−e/3) = 0

These relationships explain the electric charges of the proton and neutron within the Standard Model.

The electron's charge and the proton's charge are therefore related to different aspects of particle physics. The proton's charge follows from its quark content, while the electron has its own fundamental charge assignment.

3. The Central Research Question

Why do electrons and protons possess equal-magnitude but opposite-sign electric charges, and what underlying physical principles determine these properties?

This question can be divided into four parts:

1. What determines the electric charge assigned to elementary particles?
2. Why does the proton's composite structure produce a net charge of +e?
3. Why does the electron carry a charge of −e?
4. Can a deeper theoretical principle explain the relationship between these charge assignments?

The fourth question is especially important for the present conceptual investigation.

However, it must be distinguished from the established mathematical description of electric charge.

4. Is There a Common Underlying Basis?

Gati-Pravah Siddhant proposes investigating whether different physical properties and interactions can be examined through a common process-based perspective.

In the context of electric charge, this raises the question of whether the observed relationships between particle properties can be connected to a deeper physical principle.

The research question is not whether opposite charges exist; their existence is well established.

Nor is it whether electric charge can be explained simply by saying that particles interact. That would be insufficient.

The central issue is whether a more fundamental explanation can account for the observed charge assignments and provide a result that can be mathematically evaluated.

Any proposed explanation must reproduce established experimental results, including the observed charges of electrons, protons, and other particles.

5. Electric Charge and the Early Universe

The relationship between electric charge and the early universe introduces an additional question.

How did the charged particles and physical conditions of the early universe give rise to the matter structures observed today?

Several distinct issues must be separated:

- The fundamental charge assignments of elementary particles.
- The formation of composite particles such as protons and neutrons.
- The formation of atomic nuclei.
- The later formation of neutral atoms.
- The difference between the observed abundance of matter and antimatter.

These are related topics, but they are not the same scientific problem.

In particular, explaining the equal-magnitude opposite charges of electrons and protons does not by itself explain the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe.

The early universe and the origins of particle properties must therefore be investigated using the appropriate theoretical and observational evidence.

6. Proposed Research Method

This paper does not provide a new mathematical theory of electric charge.

A possible research programme would involve the following steps.

Step 1: Review established physics

Study electric charge, charge conservation, quantum electrodynamics, and the relevant charge assignments in the Standard Model.

Step 2: Define the proposed common basis

If Action-Flow Theory proposes a shared physical principle behind charge relationships, define that principle precisely.

Step 3: Distinguish description from explanation

Determine whether the proposal merely restates existing facts or explains why those facts hold.

Step 4: Formulate a mathematical relationship

Express the proposed principle in a form that can be compared with existing physical equations.

Step 5: Identify a testable consequence

Determine whether the proposal predicts a measurable result not already explained by established physics.

Step 6: Invite independent evaluation

Ask qualified researchers to examine the mathematics, assumptions, and compatibility with experimental evidence.

7. Limitations

This paper does not establish the ultimate origin of electric charge.

It does not claim that the electron's charge originates from the proton or that electric charge is produced by electromagnetic interactions themselves.

It also does not establish that electric charge, the strong interaction, and photon emission are manifestations of one identical physical mechanism.

Those possibilities would require precise definitions and independent evidence.

The present contribution is the formulation of a research question and a possible route toward its investigation.

Gati-Pravah Siddhant proposes examining these questions through a common process-based perspective while retaining the established distinctions between physical mechanisms.
